2026 Azerbaijani protests

The 2026 Azerbaijani protests are series of protests from 21 September in various cities and towns in Azerbaijan. They erupted in response to the ban on wearing hijab in schools by Azerbaijani government and have been described  as one of the largest anti-government protests in Azerbaijan in recent years.
Azerbaijan, although a Muslim-majority country, is a secular state, which has previously debated balancing religious freedom and secular education. The similar protests happened in 2010-2012 over restrictions on wearing headscarves in educational institutions. The Azerbaijani pro-government media defended the hijab ban by presenting it as a way to protect Azerbaijan's secular education system, while portraying the protesters as "Iranian provocateurs", linking conservative Islam to Tehran amid strained Azerbaijan–Iran relations. Public demonstrations are rare in Azerbaijan, with demonstrations unsanctioned by the government facing serious restrictions. The event strained relations with Azerbaijan's long-standing ally, Turkey, as Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan spoke against the hijab ban in unspecified country in his UN General Assembly speech immediately after the adoption of new rules in Azerbaijan, while Oktay Saral, a chief adviser to Erdoğan, and several MPs from Turkey's ruling Justice and Development Party were more explicit in their criticism of new Azerbaijani regulations. In response, the Azerbaijan's ruling New Azerbaijan Party issued a statement telling Turkey to stay out of Azerbaijan's internal affairs. The event added further tension between Azerbaijan and Turkey, with the New Azerbaijan Party linking the Turkish reaction to previous Turkish criticism of Azerbaijan's ties with Israel.

== History ==
On 21 September 2026, the Azerbaijani Cabinet of Ministers amended the rules on uniforms in public schools, prohibiting "clothing items that violate the uniform dress code or are considered inconsistent with the secular principle of general education". Although the ban did not explicitly mention hijab, although the reports suggested those girls wearing hijab were turned away from schools. In September 2026, a video circulated online alleging that the hijab had been banned in Azerbaijani schools. The video shows girls wearing the hijab being prevented from entering middle school.
On October 6, 2026, new regulations were approved for the clothing of public school teachers, “educators’ clothing must comply with the principle of the secular nature of education.” After the new rules were adopted, teachers in Baku and Sumgayit said that school administrators had asked them to remove their hijabs or resign.

2026 Poland school stabbings

Between 7 and 10 October 2026, three separate uncoordinated school stabbings were carried out at schools across the Masovian Voivodeship of Poland. The first took place in Ostrołęka on 7 October, injuring ten people, including the suspect. The second occurred on 8 October in Leszczydół-Nowiny, leaving one person dead and two others injured. The third took place in Warsaw on 10 October, three people were injured.
According to Prime Minister Donald Tusk, the attacks were motivated by nihilistic violent extremism rather than political views. Tusk also cited online glorification of violence as a contributing factor. The attacks also resulted in debate over Poland's implementation of the European Union's Digital Services Act, following President Karol Nawrocki's veto of the legislation in January.

== Background ==

School attacks are rare in Poland, although in September 2026 police detained a 13-year-old suspected of planning an attack and found a knife in his bag. That same month, an 11-year-old boy attacked a 10-year-old girl with a hatchet at a primary school in the village of Ptaki, and a 17-year-old neo-Nazi was arrested for allegedly plotting an attack on a school and a mosque.

== Attacks ==
Between 7 and 10 October 2026, three separate school stabbings were carried out at schools across Masovian Voivodeship, Poland.

=== 7 October: Ostrołęka ===
On 7 October, around 11:05 a.m. CEST, a 19-year-old student in his final year attacked nine people with a machete at Vocational School Complex No. 4 in Ostrołęka before inflicting injuries on himself and being detained by the police. The injured comprised five students, two teachers, and two bystanders. The victims were treated at the Mazovian Specialist Hospital in Ostrołęka. Police confirmed that the suspect was taken to a hospital following his arrest.
Students barricaded themselves inside classrooms before police detained the suspect about 20 minutes after the attack began. The Masovian Police released a photograph showing the suspect lying face down on the ground, with his bloodied hands handcuffed behind his back, as two officers stood nearby. According to reports, the suspect was also carrying homemade explosives in his backpack.

=== 8 October: Leszczydół-Nowiny ===
The day following the attack in Ostrołęka, another stabbing took place in Leszczydół-Nowiny. A 15-year-old graduate of the John Paul II Primary School entered the school premises armed with three knives at approximately 9:00 a.m. CEST. A 37-year-old school caretaker attempted to prevent the suspect from entering. He wounded three people, including the school caretaker, who died. The school principal, Krzysztof Ochmański, restrained the attacker, who then began apologising and assuring that he "didn't want to do it anymore", put the knives down, and asked for a hug.
According to Tusk, the suspect was wearing a helmet mounted with a camera, with which he was reportedly recording the incident, making it the first incident of its kind in Poland. The police are in possession of the recording.

=== "Bloody Friday" and other plots ===
On 8 October, reports regarding alleged planned attacks on schools began circulating widely on social media. Screenshots and recordings warning of a so-called "Bloody Friday", supposedly set for Friday, 9 October, were shared among students and parents. The police assured that they are monitoring the situation and have announced the presence of officers at schools. Police also detained a 17-year-old in Sierpc who allegedly discussed doing "something bad" at a school. Officers reportedly found two knives on the suspect and searches related to attacks on his phone. Fifty schools had all lessons called off on 9 October. Forty-five of those schools were located in the Masovian Voivodeship, three in the Świętokrzyskie Voivodeship, and one each in the Podlaskie and Warmian–Masurian Voivodeships.
On 9 October, a 14-year-old carrying a knife with the words "śmierć społeczniakom" (lit. 'death to normies') was apprehended in Mokotów, a district of Warsaw. A 16-year-old carrying a butterfly knife was taken into custody in the Warsaw district of Śródmieście the same day. Two 15-year-old students were apprehended in Pruszków after threatening the life of several people on social media, a 14-year-old in Józefów was stopped after attempting to bring a knife to school, and an inhabitant of Wiskitki Municipality was arrested in Mszczonów after threatening several people online. A 13-year-old from Płock also sent a video threatening the lives of several students from Płock schools, a 16-year-old student from Jednorożec was apprehended by Przasnysz police after telling his friends he would bring a machete to school, and a 15-year-old in Ostrów Mazowiecka stated he would kill several people.
On the afternoon of 9 October, the Ministry of the Interior and Administration released a statement that 24,000 police officers had been mobilised and 15,000 schools had been patrolled. At least 19 people had also been apprehended in connection to the attempted attacks. By 5 p.m. CEST on 9 October, 35,000 schools had been patrolled, 172 incidents were documented, and 59 people were detained. As of 11 October, police detained 117 people for carrying weapons, threatening to attack schools and students; police checked 64,000 schools.

=== 10 October: Warsaw ===
On 10 October at 12:30 p.m. CEST, a 21-year-old female student attacked three women with a knife at TEB Edukacja, an adult education school on Mińska Street in Warsaw. The victims, two aged 17 and one aged 24, were injured, with one of the 17-year-olds taken to hospital. Police officers arrived at the scene four minutes after receiving the report, and the suspect was detained by police.

== Legal proceedings and investigation ==

On 8 October, prosecutors brought nine charges against 19-year-old Jakub Z., including the attempted murder of multiple people using explosives, attempted murder with a knife, making criminal threats, and creating an immediate danger to the lives or health of multiple people. On 10 October, the court ordered the 15-year-old to be placed in a juvenile detention centre for three months, equivalent to pre-trial detention for adults. The facility is a closed institution where juveniles are supervised by educators and security staff. On 11 October, police announced that 21-year-old Kamila D. had been charged with five offenses, including the attempted murder of three people, violating the bodily integrity of a policewoman, and making criminal threats. The court ordered her to be remanded in custody for three months. During questioning at the prosecutor's office and a subsequent detention hearing, she pleaded guilty.
Tusk stated the attacks were motivated by nihilistic violent extremism, a term used by law enforcement to describe violence motivated by misanthropy and nihilism rather than political ideology. He also added that some of those detained were active in far-right circles, with the authorities observing increased online searches for Nazism-related content and mass killers who had become online idols, and attributed the attacks to the online glorification of violence.
The Polish government has so far found no evidence that the attacks were motivated by an internet challenge or that they were coordinated. According to Kacper Rekawek, a senior researcher at the International Centre for Counter-Terrorism, one of the suspected attackers used the same imagery as Brenton Tarrant, the perpetrator of the Christchurch mosque shootings.

== Reactions ==
Tusk expressed concern about the possibility of copycat crimes, citing online violence, internet challenges, and media coverage that he said promoted violence. He convened an emergency meeting on 8 October with ministers, police and security officials to discuss the school attacks. Police in northeastern Poland announced increased security checks around primary and secondary schools.
The incidents also reignited debate in Poland over the European Union's Digital Services Act (DSA). Tusk suggested that President Karol Nawrocki's January veto of legislation implementing the act had prevented authorities from obtaining tools to combat online youth radicalisation. The DSA requires online platforms to address illegal content and improve transparency. A revised implementation framework was signed in September. Jarosław Kaczyński, leader of the opposition Law and Justice party, questioned whether the incidents were connected, suggesting they could be part of a Russian hybrid warfare campaign. He cited similar attacks in Lithuania and alleged that Russian websites encouraged young people to carry out such acts.

2026 US–Denmark–Greenland agreement

The 2026 United States–Denmark–Greenland agreement (also called the Greenland security deal) is a trilateral defence agreement between the United States and the Kingdom of Denmark together with Greenland, an autonomous territory of Denmark. It was signed on 22 September 2026 by US President Donald Trump, Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen and Greenlandic Prime Minister Jens-Frederik Nielsen, at the United Nations Headquarters in New York City. It amends and supplements the existing 1951 Greenland Defense Agreement and its subsequent arrangements, expanding and clarifying the framework for the United States military presence in Greenland.
The agreement authorizes the United States to modernize and expand Pituffik Space Base and provides for additional American defence areas at Narsarsuaq and Mestersvig, while establishing a procedure for further defence areas. It also provides for accelerated consideration of unmanned military installations, broad American rights of military access, overflight and movement, including undersea movement in Greenlandic territorial waters, and measures concerning counter-espionage, sensitive foreign investment and the military presence of non-NATO states. The agreement supports an increased NATO role in the Arctic and contains provisions intended to preserve the defence arrangements if Greenland becomes independent.
The accord followed more than eight months of negotiations and a major diplomatic crisis over President Trump's demands for greater American control of Greenland, including repeated threats of annexing the territory by force. It was drafted and negotiated by a high-level working group of civil servants from all three countries, established following a meeting in Washington D.C. in January 2026 between Danish Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen, Greenlandic minister of foreign affairs Vivian Motzfeldt, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio and US Vice President JD Vance, during the height of the crisis. In the final agreement, the US reaffirms the sovereignty and territorial integrity of the Kingdom of Denmark, as well as the right to self-determination of the people of Greenland.
It is expected to enter into force, when it has been ratified by both the Folketing (parliament of Denmark) and Inatsisartut (parliament of Greenland), and the United States receives a diplomatic note confirming this. Legally considered an executive agreement of the United States, it does not require ratification by two-thirds of the Senate, as otherwise stipulated by the Treaty Clause of the US Constitution.

== Provisions ==
The agreement amends the 1951 Defense Agreement and later Defense Arrangements, including the 2004 Igaliku Agreement. Britain's House of Commons Library describes the agreement as an update of the 1951 one, as much of that original agreement's core substance remains, with the new 2026 agreement only supplementing and replacing a few sections. The agreement has no stated end date and may be amended only by mutual consent of all parties, under Article XIII of the original 1951 agreement. The Centre for Eastern Studies wrote that the text largely upholds the previous status quo and the pre-existing trilateral cooperation in the context of NATO.
The preamble respects and reaffirms the self-determination of the people of Greenland as a people under international law with the right to self-determination and the sovereignty and territorial integrity of the Kingdom of Denmark. Also noted is the procedure for Greenlandic independence is set in Article 21 of the Act on Greenland Self-Government. After the signing of the agreement, Denmark Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen said that the agreement respects and acknowledges both Greenlandic and Danish rights, sovereignty, and territorial integrity. Reuters and others note that the accord does not fulfill President Trump's stated intention to annex Greenland as a US territory.

=== Defense areas and access ===
Per Article IV, the US is permitted to expand its operations at the Pituffik Space Base and to establish an additional "Defense Area" at Narsarsuaq and Mestersvig, with certain details to be mutually agreed upon at a later date. Either party may propose further Defense Areas. The US is required to preferentially award commercial contracts for the building and maintenance of these Defense Areas to Greenlandic firms to the fullest extent reasonably possible. Article V outlines a process for accelerated review of requests to construct unmanned military installations outside Defense Areas.
Article VI of the agreement gives free access to and movement between Defense Areas in Greenland and its territorial waters to United States public vessels, aircraft, armed forces, and other vehicles. United states aircraft are also allowed to fly over and land anywhere in Greenland, and US public vessels are allowed full undersea access and movement, except for any restriction which the parties mutually agree upon. Stars and Stripes wrote that the flight and free movement rights are just reinstatements of the original 1951 agreement. These rights are subject to the restriction that they are to be exercised in a manner that respects Greenlandic society and their way of life, especially hunting and fishing, to the maximum extent possible. The Centre for Eastern Studies' report describes the framework as including environmental protection provisions and guardrails to protect the traditional Inuit way of life.
The preamble of the agreement states a shared interest in United States military access "including through establishment of a Golden Dome defense system". The articles do not specify the weapons or other systems that would be placed in Greenland.

=== Third state forces and investment screening ===
Article VII specifies that territory near a Defense Area will not be used in any way that threatens the area's security, and that the parties will work together in counterespionage operations. Article VIII states that Denmark has strengthened, and will continue to strengthen its Arctic security posture and its cooperation with NATO allies.

== Implementation ==
Article XII makes entry into force dependent on completion of the necessary parliamentary procedures in Denmark and Greenland followed by a diplomatic note to the United States. Danish foreign minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen informed the Folketing on 22 September that the agreement would therefore have to be considered by both the Folketing and Inatsisartut.

United Nations General Assembly Resolution 2832 (XXVI)

United Nations General Assembly Resolution 2832 (XXVI), the Declaration of the Indian Ocean as a Zone of Peace, was adopted by the United Nations General Assembly on 16 December 1971. It declared that the Indian Ocean "is hereby designated for all time as a zone of peace". It called on the great powers to consult the littoral states with a view to halting the expansion of their military presence in the ocean, and to eliminating "bases, military installations and logistical supply facilities" and nuclear weapons conceived in the context of great-power rivalry. The item was proposed by Ceylon, following a call by the Non-Aligned Movement in 1970. The resolution was adopted by 61 votes to none, with 55 abstentions, including those of the United Kingdom, the United States, the Soviet Union and France.
The declaration was adopted in the year in which the main forcible removal of the inhabitants of Diego Garcia, in the British Indian Ocean Territory, took place, in July and September 1971. Mauritius and its supporters later invoked the zone of peace against the base and in support of Mauritius' claim to the Chagos Archipelago. A 1975 United States Department of State paper noted that keeping the British Indian Ocean Territory intact would show the American intention "to reject the Zone of Peace concept". The General Assembly has continued to adopt resolutions on implementing the declaration, most recently in 2025.

== Background ==

At the Third Conference of Heads of State or Government of Non-Aligned Countries, held at Lusaka from 8 to 10 September 1970, the participants called on all states "to consider and respect the Indian Ocean as a zone of peace", from which great-power rivalries and the bases conceived in the context of such rivalries "should be excluded", and declared that the area should be free of nuclear weapons.
The United Kingdom had detached the Chagos Archipelago from Mauritius in 1965 to form the British Indian Ocean Territory. On 30 December 1966 it concluded an agreement with the United States making the territory available for the defence purposes of both governments. An Agreed Minute of the same date provided that the British administrative measures would include "resettling any inhabitants". On 16 April 1971 the BIOT Commissioner enacted an immigration ordinance making it unlawful to enter or remain in the archipelago without a permit. Between 1967 and 1973 the entire population was either prevented from returning or forcibly removed. The main removal from Diego Garcia took place in July and September 1971.

== Drafting and adoption ==
By a letter of 1 October 1971, Ceylon asked for an item entitled "Declaration of the Indian Ocean as a zone of peace" to be added to the agenda of the 26th session; Tanzania joined the request on 6 October. The Assembly allocated the item to the First Committee, which debated it together with six disarmament items from 11 November to 1 December 1971.
The draft resolution was submitted by Ceylon, Iran, Iraq, Kenya, Somalia, Uganda, Tanzania, Yemen and Zambia, and was later co-sponsored by Burundi and Swaziland. After revision, India and Yugoslavia also joined, and the representative of Ceylon introduced the final 13-power text on 10 December 1971. At the request of the Philippines and Madagascar, the committee voted separately on parts of the text. It adopted operative paragraph 1 by 47 votes to none, with 46 abstentions, and paragraphs 2 and 3 by 43 to none, with 55 abstentions. It adopted the draft as a whole by a roll-call vote of 50 to none, with 49 abstentions.
The General Assembly considered the First Committee's report at its 2022nd plenary meeting on 16 December 1971, under the presidency of Adam Malik of Indonesia. It again voted separately on the operative paragraphs, adopting paragraph 1 by 60 votes to none, with 55 abstentions, and paragraphs 2 and 3 by 52 votes to none, with 63 abstentions. It adopted the resolution as a whole by 61 votes to none, with 55 abstentions. Iraq and Sierra Leone later informed the Secretariat that they had wished to vote in favour.

Mauritius does not appear among the states recorded as voting in the committee or in plenary.

== Content ==
The preamble records the determination of the peoples of the littoral and hinterland states of the Indian Ocean to preserve their independence, sovereignty and territorial integrity. It recalls the Lusaka declaration, and expresses concern at "recent developments that portend the extension of the arms race into the Indian Ocean area". In the operative part, the General Assembly:

"solemnly declares that the Indian Ocean, within limits to be determined, together with the air space above and the ocean floor subjacent thereto is hereby designated for all time as a zone of peace";
calls on the great powers to enter into immediate consultations with the littoral states with a view to halting "the further escalation and expansion of their military presence in the Indian Ocean", and eliminating from it "all bases, military installations and logistical supply facilities", nuclear weapons and weapons of mass destruction, and "any manifestation of great Power military presence in the Indian Ocean conceived in the context of great Power rivalry";
calls on the littoral and hinterland states, the permanent members of the Security Council and other major maritime users of the ocean to consult on implementing the declaration, so that warships and military aircraft may not use the ocean for any threat or use of force against a littoral or hinterland state. Subject to that, "the right to free and unimpeded use of the zone by the vessels of all nations is unaffected";
asks the Secretary-General to report on progress at the 27th session; and
places the item on the agenda of that session.

== Follow-up ==
By resolution 2992 (XXVII) of 15 December 1972, the General Assembly noted that the consultations called for in 1971 "have not taken place". It established an Ad Hoc Committee on the Indian Ocean, of no more than fifteen members, to study the implications of the proposal. A meeting of the littoral and hinterland states of the Indian Ocean was held in New York in July 1979.
The Ad Hoc Committee still existed in the 2020s. In 2023 it had 43 members, among them Mauritius, the Maldives and Seychelles, with Sri Lanka holding the chair; the United Kingdom and the United States were not members. The General Assembly adopted resolutions on implementing the declaration every two years from 1999. Resolution 80/13 of 1 December 2025 asked the committee's chair to continue informal consultations and to report to the Assembly at its 82nd session.

=== Mauritian and international invocation ===
In 1974, the Mauritian Prime Minister, Seewoosagur Ramgoolam, referred in a speech to "rumours of a naval base at Diego Garcia, which Mauritius gave up prior to its Independence", and said: "We want the Indian Ocean to be a zone of peace". At the General Assembly in September 1974, Mauritius said that any British and American decision to extend facilities on Diego Garcia "would constitute a flagrant violation of the United Nations resolution on the subject". In 1982 Prime Minister Anerood Jugnauth told the Assembly that Diego Garcia was "now a full-fledged nuclear base". He called it a threat to the security of Mauritius and of the states of "the very Ocean declared to be a zone of peace by this Assembly in 1971".
In July 1980 the Organisation of African Unity adopted resolution AHG/Res. 99 (XVII) on Diego Garcia. Declaring that "the militarization of Diego Garcia is a threat to Africa, and to the Indian Ocean as a zone of peace", it demanded that the island "be unconditionally returned to Mauritius and that its peaceful character be maintained". Judge Antônio Augusto Cançado Trindade recalled the resolution in his separate opinion on the 2019 advisory opinion. At its 1983 summit in New Delhi, the Non-Aligned Movement said that "the establishment and strengthening of the military base at Diego Garcia has endangered the sovereignty, territorial integrity and peaceful development of Mauritius and other States". At a 1983 meeting in the General Assembly on the Ad Hoc Committee's report, the representatives of Mozambique and the Soviet Union described Diego Garcia as territory taken from Mauritius.
In its 2013 counter-memorial in the Mauritius v United Kingdom arbitration, the United Kingdom noted that Mauritius' statements in the General Assembly's general debate between 1982 and 1998 had referred to the archipelago with particular reference to military bases and the Assembly's Indian Ocean zone of peace resolutions. It argued that such speeches "were consistent with present United Kingdom sovereignty". In the 2018 advisory proceedings, it cited Ramgoolam's 1974 speech as evidence that he had regarded the detachment as agreed, noting that the speech made "no claim to Mauritian sovereignty over the Archipelago".

=== American assessment, 1975 ===
In October 1975 the State Department's Bureau of Politico-Military Affairs examined whether Aldabra, Farquhar and Desroches should be returned to Seychelles on its independence. It noted that the zone of peace proposal, "sparked by Sri Lanka", had "gained considerable support among the littoral countries". The paper stated that the United States had opposed the concept, which embodied the principle that littoral states could regulate the use of an international body of water, and that the basic concept of the British Indian Ocean Territory was contrary to the zone of peace programme. It observed that, publicly at least, the Mauritian Prime Minister sided with India and other littoral countries in opposing the development of military facilities on Diego Garcia. The paper weighed the option of opposing the islands' return. It concluded that keeping them would show the intention "to maintain our Indian Ocean presence and to reject the Zone of Peace concept". It judged that such a stance would probably provoke "resignation and antagonism in equal degree among the littoral countries". The three islands were returned to Seychelles on its independence on 29 June 1976.

Anatole Von Lilienfeld

Otto Anatole von Lilienfeld-Toal  is a computational chemist whose research focuses on quantum chemistry, machine learning, and materials discovery. He is a professor at the University of Toronto, where he holds the Ed Clark Chair in Advanced Materials, and a Canada CIFAR AI Chair affiliated with the Vector Institute. He has also held academic appointments at the University of Basel and the University of Vienna.
Von Lilienfeld is known for his work on applying machine learning methods to chemical and materials science problems, particularly the prediction of molecular properties and the exploration of chemical compound space. He has served in editorial roles for several scientific journals, including as founding editor-in-chief of Machine Learning: Science and Technology and chief editor of PRX Intelligence.

== Education ==
Von Lilienfeld studied chemistry at the University of Leipzig, the École européenne de chimie, polymères et matériaux in Strasbourg, and ETH Zürich. He completed his doctoral studies in computational chemistry at the École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL) in 2005 under the supervision of Ursula Röthlisberger.
Following his doctorate, he held research appointments at New York University and the Max Planck Institute for Polymer Research with support from the Swiss National Science Foundation.

In 2007, von Lilienfeld joined Sandia National Laboratories as a Truman Fellow and later became a member of the technical staff. In 2011, he moved to Argonne National Laboratory, where he worked at the Argonne Leadership Computing Facility.
From 2013 to 2020, he held faculty positions at the University of Basel, including appointments as assistant professor and associate professor. During this period he also held a visiting appointment at the Free University of Brussels. He subsequently became Professor of Computational Materials Discovery at the University of Vienna.
In 2022, von Lilienfeld joined the University of Toronto as the Ed Clark Chair in Advanced Materials. He is also affiliated with the Vector Institute, where he was named a Canada CIFAR AI Chair in 2023.
In addition to his research activities, von Lilienfeld has served on the editorial boards of several scientific journals. In 2020, von Lilienfeld became the founding editor-in-chief of Machine Learning: Science and Technology, a multidisciplinary journal published by IOP Publishing that focuses on applications of machine learning across the sciences.
In 2026, he became the editor of PRX Intelligence, writing in inaugural editorial that the journal would focus on artificial intelligence and machine learning applications in the physical sciences.

== Research ==
Von Lilienfeld's research includes contributions around quantum chemistry, machine learning, and materials science. His work has focused on the use of statistical-learning methods to predict molecular and materials properties, explore chemical compound space, and accelerate computational approaches to chemical discovery. He has contributed to the development of machine-learning models for molecular property prediction and to quantum-chemistry datasets, including the widely used QM9 dataset. His research has also addressed the development of transferable and data-efficient machine-learning methods for applications across diverse chemical systems.
A 2020 article by Chemical & Engineering News profiled von Lilienfeld's work applying artificial-intelligence methods to the exploration of chemical space, an approach intended to assist the discovery of new molecules and materials by computationally evaluating large numbers of candidate compounds. Von Lilienfeld has described machine learning as complementing experiment, theory, and computation in the physical sciences, and has advocated the integration of experimental and computational data to improve predictive modelling and scientific discovery.

Green Deal Ukraïna

Green Deal Ukraïna (GDU) is a German–Polish–Ukrainian project focused on Ukraine's energy and climate transition, energy-system security and resilience and integration into the European Union. Launched in 2023, the four-year project is coordinated by Helmholtz-Zentrum Berlin (HZB) and funded by the German Federal Ministry of Research, Technology and Space. Its work combines energy-system modelling and data analysis, policy research and advice, capacity building and institution-building, with a particular focus on supporting Ukraine's energy security, reconstruction and integration into the European Union. The project also supported the establishment of the Kyiv Energy and Climate Lab (KECLab), a joint scientific lab of HZB and the National University of Kyiv-Mohyla Academy.

== History and development ==
Green Deal Ukraïna began on 1 June 2023 as a four-year initiative coordinated by Helmholtz-Zentrum Berlin in cooperation with partners in Poland and Ukraine. The project was established in the context of Ukraine's wartime energy challenges, reconstruction needs and preparations for European Union membership.
In its first phase, GDU developed research and advisory activities on Ukraine's energy system, electricity market reform, decarbonisation, and EU energy law alignment. In parallel, it worked towards establishing a permanent research and education institution in Kyiv.
In June 2025, HZB and the National University of Kyiv-Mohyla Academy signed a memorandum of understanding on long-term cooperation in energy and climate research. In November 2025, the university established the Kyiv Energy and Climate Lab (KECLab) as a joint scientific centre with HZB. In May 2026, cooperation around KECLab expanded to include Polish research and policy institutions, strengthening the German–Polish–Ukrainian dimension of the initiative.

== Goals and key objectives ==
The project is organised around three principal areas of work:

energy-system modelling and data analysis;
policy analysis and advice on energy and climate policy;
education, capacity building and institutional development.
Across these areas, GDU addresses the interaction between Ukraine's immediate energy-security needs, post-war reconstruction and the longer-term transformation required by EU integration and climate policy.

== Organisation ==
The project is coordinated by Helmholtz-Zentrum Berlin and funded by the German BMFTR, with the Polish partner being funded through its own funds.
Partner organisations in the Project Coordination Group are Forum Energii in Poland and DiXi Group and Ecoaction in Ukraine. Cooperation on training, research and policy activities has also involved other European and Ukrainian institutions.
The project is supported by an international Advisory Council of experts from European and Ukrainian energy policy, regulation, academia and research, finance and energy infrastructure. Its members include former government ministers and public officials, current and former heads of energy-sector institutions and regulatory bodies, academics and researchers, and executives of European energy and financial organisations. The Council provides strategic guidance on the project's research and policy priorities.

== Activities ==
Green Deal Ukraïna's activities are organised around three main areas.

=== Data and modelling ===
Green Deal Ukraïna develops quantitative models and datasets for analysing Ukraine's energy system and its integration with the European energy market. The project uses modelling tools including PyPSA (Python for Power System Analysis) for electricity-system modelling and LEAP (Low Emissions Analysis Platform) for longer-term energy and emissions scenarios. Its PyPSA-based models represent electricity generation, transmission, consumption and cross-border flows and have been used to study issues such as electricity imports, system adequacy and demand-side responses. GDU also maintains an online dashboard tracking generation availability, cross-border electricity flows, outages, day-ahead prices and gas-storage levels.

=== Research and policy analysis ===
GDU conducts research on Ukraine's electricity and gas systems, energy security, energy-sector governance and regulation, renewable energy, electricity-market integration, carbon pricing, decarbonisation and implementation of the EU energy and climate acquis.
External institutions have cited or used project analysis on topics including electricity-system resilience, Ukraine–EU grid integration, biomethane and energy reconstruction. In 2025, the European Bank for Reconstruction and Development stated that input from Green Deal Ukraïna and Ukrainian renewable-energy associations had contributed to the development of a planned risk-mitigation mechanism for renewable-energy investment in Ukraine.

=== Capacity building ===
Green Deal Ukraïna develops capacity-building and educational programmes for public officials, regulators, researchers, local authorities, students and energy-sector professionals, with a particular focus on the knowledge and analytical capabilities required for Ukraine's energy transition and implementation of the EU energy and climate acquis.
Since 2024, the project has implemented several training programmes, including ProGreenDeal for Policymakers, ProGreenDeal for Local Communities and ProGreenDeal: EU Accession, as well as a self-paced online course on climate policy integration in Ukraine. Participants have included representatives of Ukrainian ministries, the energy regulator, parliament, local governments, academia, think tanks, civil society and the private sector.
The programmes combine online learning and expert lectures with case studies, mentoring, simulations and in-person workshops. GDU also develops institution-specific training on demand. These activities are carried out in cooperation with institutions, including the Florence School of Regulation.
The establishment of KECLab expanded this work towards longer-term academic education and institutional capacity building in Ukraine.

== Kyiv Energy and Climate Lab ==
The Kyiv Energy and Climate Lab (KECLab) was established in November 2025 as a joint scientific laboratory of Helmholtz-Zentrum Berlin and the National University of Kyiv-Mohyla Academy.
KECLab was established to develop long-term research and education capacity in Ukraine in the fields of energy and climate policy. KECLab conducts research on energy and climate policy, including energy-system transformation and modelling, electricity markets, renewable energy, energy security, decarbonisation and Ukraine's integration into the European Union. It also develops educational programmes, including a planned master's programme in Energy and Climate and academic exchanges.

== Partnerships ==
Green Deal Ukraïna collaborates with governmental institutions, universities, research organisations and international partners in the EU, internationally and in several European countries, with foremost Germany, Poland and Ukraine. Its partners include the National University of Kyiv-Mohyla Academy, Forum Energii, DiXi Group, Ecoaction, the Florence School of Regulation, and the Kyiv School of Energy Policy.

Lachit Borphukan Maidam

The Lachit Borphukan Maidam, also referred to as the Lachit Maidam, is the maidam (burial mound) of Lachit Borphukan, a commander of the Ahom kingdom, and the memorial complex built around it. It is located at Hollongapar in the Meleng area near Teok in Jorhat district, Assam, India. The maidam dates from the Ahom period, following Lachit Borphukan's death in 1672, while the surrounding memorial complex was developed by the Government of Assam in the 2020s.
The site is also the location of the Statue of Valour, a 125 feet (38 m) bronze statue of Lachit Borphukan, which was unveiled by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on 9 March 2024 as part of the Lachit Barphukan Maidam Development Project. The state government is developing the site as a memorial and tourist destination. The government has also marked Lachit Divas, observed in Assam on 24 November in honour of Lachit Borphukan, at the site.

== Background ==

Lachit Borphukan was a military commander of the Ahom kingdom, which ruled much of the Brahmaputra Valley from 1228 to 1826. He is known for his leadership in the 1671 Battle of Saraighat, in which Ahom forces thwarted an attempt by a Mughal army led by Raja Ram Singh I to retake Assam. He died a year later, at the age of 49, of illness.

== Description ==
The maidam is situated at Gohain Gaon in Meleng-Hulungapar, about 8 kilometres (5.0 mi) from the Hoollongapar Gibbon Sanctuary. As of April 2024, the Statue of Valour stood in a park under construction that includes the maidam.

=== Ahom period ===
The Ahoms traditionally buried their kings, queens and nobles in moidams (or maidams), earthen mounds raised over a burial chamber. Charaideo, the first capital of the Ahom kingdom, became the principal royal necropolis. Under Hindu influence, the Ahoms later adopted cremation, although burial continued among certain Ahom priestly groups.
Lachit Borphukan was ill during the Battle of Saraighat, which ended in 1671. After the battle, the Ahom king Udayaditya Singha summoned him to Sivasagar for the victory celebrations, and he travelled up the Brahmaputra towards upper Assam. He died on 25 April 1672 on a boat between Kaliabor and Dergaon.
Charaideo was reserved for members of the royal family, while generals and nobles were buried in separate maidams. As a member of Lachit's family was living at Hollongapar, his funeral rites were performed there. His remains were laid under the maidam by Udayaditya Singha, which was constructed in 1672. The site has since been referred to as the Samadhi Khetra of Lachit Borphukan.

=== Commemoration and preservation ===
On 24 November 2016, Chief Minister Sarbananda Sonowal visited the maidam as part of Lachit Divas observances; it was the first time the state government had celebrated the day at the burial site. Sonowal said the State Archaeology Department would take steps to protect the site as a place of historical and archaeological importance.
On 21 November 2021, Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma visited the maidam and announced ₹1 crore for its preservation and development.

=== Redevelopment ===
On 25 February 2022, President Ram Nath Kovind launched a year-long commemoration of the 400th birth anniversary of Lachit Borphukan at Guwahati and, by video link, laid the foundation stone for beautification and repair work at the maidam in Jorhat. At the same event, plans were announced for a memorial complex at Hollongapar, to be developed over 50 bighas of land with an auditorium, guest houses, a museum and a refreshment hall, and a 150 feet (46 m) bronze statue of Lachit Borphukan. The plans also included development of a pond near the maidam.
In November 2022, as the state government concluded the year-long anniversary celebrations, work on the memorial was scheduled to begin on 20 November with the handing over of compensation cheques to landowners for land acquisition. The statue was now planned to be 125 feet (38 m) tall, at a cost of ₹175 crore. On 21 November 2022, the construction company Ahluwalia Contracts (India) received an order worth ₹120.23 crore from the Government of Assam to build the Lachit Moidam Memorial and Cultural Complex at Jorhat.
Before the redevelopment, the maidam site covered about four bighas. The state government added roughly 46 to 47 bighas for the new complex, with land handed over by villagers and compensation paid to the landowners.

=== Statue of Valour ===

The statue was installed in the first phase of the development project. It was designed by the sculptor Ram V. Sutar, who also designed the Statue of Unity, and was transported in parts from Uttar Pradesh and assembled at the site. The figure is 84 feet (26 m) tall and stands on a 41 feet (12 m) pedestal, giving a total height of 125 feet (38 m). Narendra Modi unveiled it on 9 March 2024, taking part in an Ahom ritual during the ceremony.

=== Second phase ===
At the time of the unveiling, work on the second phase was under way, including an auditorium with a capacity of 500. The total cost of the project is ₹214 crore. On 13 December 2024, Chief Minister Sarma inspected progress on the second phase, including a collection centre, an auditorium and an indoor stadium; the maidam and cultural complex was then expected to open in 2025.

== Lachit Barphukan Maidam Development Project ==

The Lachit Barphukan Maidam Development Project covers about 16.5 acres (6.7 ha) at Hollongapar and is intended to develop the area as a tourist destination highlighting the history of the region. Besides the statue, the project includes a museum, a 500-seat auditorium and a hostel. The plans also include two museums and accommodation for research scholars.

Bo Heinebäck

Kurt Axel Bo "Bosse" Heinebäck (2 March 1937 – 19 September 2021) was a Swedish diplomat who served in several diplomatic posts during his career. He joined the Swedish Ministry for Foreign Affairs in 1964 and held postings in Algiers and Washington, D.C. From 1977 to 1980, he served at Sweden's Permanent Mission to the United Nations in New York City, where he focused on Southern African affairs and decolonization.
In 1980, Heinebäck became Sweden's ambassador to Zimbabwe, serving until 1984. He subsequently held senior positions at the Ministry for Foreign Affairs, including director of a division responsible for Asian affairs and head of press. In 1990, he was appointed ambassador to Australia, with concurrent responsibility for several Pacific island states. From 1994, he served as ambassador to South Africa, also assuming responsibility for Mauritius in 1999.
After leaving his embassy post in 2000, Heinebäck moved to Botswana, where he advised former president Quett Masire. He participated in the Inter-Congolese Dialogue, an initiative aimed at resolving the Second Congo War, until 2002.

== Early life ==
Heinebäck was born on 2 March 1937 in his aunt's house in Motala Parish, in Motala, Sweden, to Carl Axel Heinebäck (1910–1991), a police inspector, and his wife, Ingrid Larsson (1915–1987), who worked as a domestic assistant. He had four brothers, one older and two younger. He grew up with his family in Mjölby and Eksjö, in the province of Småland, where he spent his school years. In his youth, he enjoyed gymnastics and athletics. At the age of 20, he completed his compulsory military service with the Göta Engineer Corps in Eksjö.
He graduated from Uppsala University with a Candidate of Law degree in 1963. Alongside his studies, he served in 1962 as a personnel welfare officer with the Swedish United Nations forces deployed to the United Nations Operation in the Congo (ONUC) in the Republic of the Congo.

== Career ==
In September 1964, Heinebäck was selected by the Ministry for Foreign Affairs' training board as one of ten candidates for its newly established paid diplomatic training programme, which replaced the previous system of recruiting attachés. He was admitted to the same cohort as Bo Henrikson, Ingolf Kiesow, Lars Norberg, and Ulf Schöldström. In June 1967, he was appointed embassy secretary in Algiers. In June 1969, he was assigned to serve in Washington, D.C., and in October 1971, he was promoted to first embassy secretary.
From 1973 to 1975, he worked at the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI). He then returned to the ministry, serving as a desk officer (departementssekreterare) in Stockholm. Between 1975 and 1977, he worked in the ministry's division responsible for Africa and the Middle East. In March 1976, Heinebäck joined a five-member delegation from the Ministry for Foreign Affairs and the Swedish Trade Council that travelled to Angola, just months after the country gained independence, to explore opportunities for a major new Swedish export initiative. In August 1977, he participated in the Swedish delegation to the World Conference for Action against Apartheid in Lagos, Nigeria.
In September 1977, Heinebäck was appointed first embassy secretary at Sweden's Permanent Mission to the United Nations in New York City. There, he served, among other roles, as an alternate member of the United Nations General Assembly Fourth Committee. From September 1977 to March 1980, he specialised in Southern African affairs and decolonization at the Swedish UN representation. He also served as Sweden's expert on a United Nations working group on Southern Africa.
In March 1980, Sweden planned to seek accreditation for a chargé d'affaires in time for Zimbabwe's independence celebrations, with the intention of opening an embassy in the country shortly thereafter. That same month, Heinebäck took charge of operations at the Swedish liaison office in Salisbury. Sweden recognised Zimbabwe as a sovereign and independent state on 18 April 1980, the day of its independence. Diplomatic relations were established immediately, and Heinebäck had already been nominated as Sweden's ambassador to the new state. He served as ambassador in Harare from 1980 to 1984.
After four years in Zimbabwe, Heinebäck was appointed director (departementsråd) at the Ministry for Foreign Affairs' Political Department, Division Four, which handled Asian affairs. He served as a director from 1984 to 1986 and as head of press at the ministry from 1986 to 1990. In 1990, he was appointed ambassador to Canberra, with concurrent responsibilities for Port Vila and Honiara. In 1991, he also became responsible for relations with Port Moresby. From 1994, he served as ambassador to Pretoria, and from 1999, he also held responsibility for Port Louis.
During his posting to South Africa, Heinebäck was involved in the case of Craig Williamson. In 1996, Swedish investigators travelled to South Africa to investigate the South African connection in the inquiry into the assassination of Olof Palme. Among the activities was an interrogation of Eugene de Kock at his prison in Pretoria by Tommy Lindström, a Swedish investigator. In February 1997, Heinebäck was responsible for arrangements surrounding the Swedish royal couple's state visit to South Africa. He also handled matters relating to the sale of the Saab JAS 39 Gripen fighter aircraft to South Africa and Swedish prime minister Göran Persson's visit to the country in November 1999.
Heinebäck's final working day at the embassy was 5 September 2000. He subsequently moved to Gaborone, Botswana, where he became a close adviser to former Botswana president Quett Masire. Masire had been appointed by other Southern African countries to facilitate negotiations among all parties involved in the Second Congo War. Heinebäck concluded his assignment with the Inter-Congolese Dialogue in 2002.

== Personal life ==
In March 1972, Heinebäck married Barbara Taylor (born 1944), originally from Georgia, in Washington, D.C.. At the time, she was a production secretary for the CBS programme Face the Nation. She later became the first African American woman to serve as a press officer to former First Lady Rosalynn Carter. The couple divorced in 1976. They had one son, Erik (born 1973).
In April 1994, Heinebäck married Katarina Landgren. They had one son, Carl (born 1995). Upon his retirement in 2002, Heinebäck returned from South Africa to Australia with his family and settled in Canberra. He and Katarina divorced in 2014.
During his retirement and final years, Heinebäck divided his time between Sweden and Australia, where his sons lived, until the COVID-19 pandemic disrupted his annual travels. He owned a summer house, Lövbacka, just south of Myresjö, in the province of Småland.

== Death ==
Heinebäck died on 19 September 2021 in Sollentuna. The funeral service was held on 22 October 2021 at Saint Lawrence Chapel (S:t Lars kapell) in Eksjö. He was buried in his parents' grave at Skogskyrkogården in Eksjö on 1 July 2022.

Ultimate unification

Ultimate unification, also called eventual unification, is a discourse on the future direction of cross-strait relations that promotes Chinese unification in the long-term under certain conditions. It posits that the Republic of China (ROC), which currently governs Taiwan, Penghu, Kinmen, and Matsu, and the People's Republic of China (PRC), which governs mainland China, are "one China, two political entities". While the current situation is characterized by two Chinas due to China's division and separate governance, the supporters of ultimate unification believe that at some point in the future, under certain conditions, they should be unified into one China. This concept originates from the ROC's "One China" policy. In the Guidelines for National Unification passed by the Lee Teng-hui administration, ultimate unification replaced the previous Chiang Ching-kuo administration's "Three Principles of the People Unifying China" policy, becoming one of the ROC government's discourses on cross-strait policy.
The ultimate unification theory presupposes that the People's Republic of China is independent and no longer part of the Republic of China. Its ultimate goal is the political unification of Taiwan and mainland China. It does not support maintaining the status quo across the Taiwan Strait permanently, but it avoids the question of when and how unification will begin, essentially supporting not setting an end date for the status quo. Proponents of ultimate unification oppose the "one country on each side" approach and other policies promoting Taiwan independence, but they also oppose direct political negotiations and unification under the current circumstances. They set conditions, hoping for unification after mainland China has undergone social liberalization and democratization. Before mainland China undergoes a democratic transition, they oppose setting a timetable for an urgent unification proposal.
The relative concept of ultimate unification is "ultimate independence", which is one of the traditional arguments for Taiwan independence. It means that at some point in the future, and under certain conditions, the Republic of China system that currently exists in Taiwan will be replaced by a new Republic of Taiwan, thus achieving true Taiwan independence.

== Background ==
After the Chinese Civil War, the Republic of China government, led by the Kuomintang, retreated to Taiwan, while the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) established the People's Republic of China (PRC) on the mainland. Both sides believed that only they could represent the legitimate China and denied the legitimacy of the other's existence.
In 1971, the RAND Corporation published "Remaking China Policy", advocating "One China, but not now". Under this policy, Taiwan was considered Chinese territory, not currently part of the People's Republic of China, but potentially to be unified with China in the future.

In Taiwan, proponents of ultimate unification often cite the Constitution of the Republic of China and its Additional Articles as the basis for Taiwan's pursuit of ultimate unification. During President Lee Teng-hui's term, the 1991 Guidelines for National Unification were adopted, aiming for Chinese unification. However, he believed this goal would be achieved in three phases, without a time limit, but ultimately, both sides would reunify. In his book, Taiwan's Claim, Lee Teng-hui stated:China should be unified, but it must be unified under a democratic, free, and equitable system that takes into account the interests of all Chinese people and conforms to the world trend, rather than under the communist system that has been proven to fail in practice or the so-called "one country, two systems". Compared with the "counterattack the mainland" during Chiang Kai-shek's era or the Three Principles of the People's policy of unifying China during Chiang Ching-kuo's era, the ultimate unification does not emphasize the initiative of Taiwan to unify China. The stance of this program was later interpreted as ultimate unification.
In July 1999, Lee Teng-hui proposed that the cross-strait relationship was "at least a special relationship between two countries." This view, known as the "two-states theory", did not support or oppose ultimate unification. Based on the idea of ​​the two-states theory, the Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) further proposed the Resolution on Taiwan's Future, advocating Taiwan independence and that Taiwan's future be determined by the Taiwanese people themselves. During the DPP's administration from 2000 to 2008, the Republic of China government no longer pursued ultimate unification, abolished the National Unification Council and the Guidelines for National Unification, but made the Four Noes and One Without without guarantee. After the Kuomintang returned to power in 2008, its view on ultimate unification became more low-key, emphasizing "one China with respective interpretations", the "1992 Consensus", the "one China" principle in the Constitution, and "no unification, no independence, no use of force". In 2015, Hung Hsiu-chu intended to run for the 2016 presidential election. After Hung Hsiu-chu put forward the argument that the Republic of China Constitution supported ultimate unification, because it was believed that her cross-strait views might endanger the election, a controversy arose over replacing her. The Kuomintang eventually changed its presidential candidate to Eric Chu.

=== Early stage ===
When Lee Teng-hui was president of the Republic of China, he repeatedly vowed to pursue the unification of China. For example, on 13 April 1989, when Lee Teng-hui received French visitors, he said: "There is only one China, and it will definitely be unified in the future." However, he then added that because the political systems on both sides of the Taiwan Strait are different, unification is not possible in the short term. He hoped that unification would only take place after the mainland has achieved a free, democratic, and equitable society. In the later period of his administration, the "two-states theory" emerged.

=== Chen Shui-bian administration ===
On 25 October 2004, United States secretary of state Colin Powell visited China and was interviewed by CNN and Phoenix Television. During the interview, he mentioned that Taiwan does not enjoy national sovereignty and is not a sovereign independent country. He also expressed his hope that the two sides of the Taiwan Strait would reach a peaceful reunification after negotiations. Subsequently, the U.S. State Department issued a statement saying that it was a slip of the tongue. The Taiwan Affairs Office said it believes that this is in line with the U.S. position on the one-China principle.
In 2005, the 10th National People's Congress passed the Anti-Secession Law, declaring its determination to pursue the unification of China. In the same year, Ma Ying-jeou, who was then the chairman of the Kuomintang, said in an interview with Newsweek that the ultimate goal of the Kuomintang was to pursue unification. At that time, Wang Jin-pyng, the President of the Legislative Yuan, believed that this was Ma Ying-jeou's personal opinion and did not represent the overall position of the Kuomintang. In response to Ma Ying-jeou's remarks, the Chen Shui-bian government proposed to abolish the Guidelines for National Unification and the National Unification Council. In January 2006, Ma Ying-jeou had a dinner with Japanese media and explained the theory of ultimate unification, believing that it was in line with the One-China Constitution and the Guidelines for National Unification, and that the main goal was to maintain the status quo. Subsequently, during his visit to the United States and Europe in February, he avoided talking about the topic of ultimate unification and stopping the civil war, and once again positioned the pursuit of ultimate unification as maintaining the status quo and keeping Chen Shui-bian's Four Noes and One Without, in order to eliminate the doubts of the United States and Europe about the theory of ultimate unification. Equating ultimate unification with maintaining the status quo rather than actively pursuing unification became the official position of the Kuomintang.
In 2007, entrepreneur Robert Tsao published an article advocating for the passage of the Cross-Strait Peaceful Coexistence Act in Taiwan to promote a referendum on unification. He was criticized by President Chen Shui-bian and the DPP Legislative Yuan caucus for his stance being the same as that of Hu Jintao, the General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party, and Ma Ying-jeou, the Kuomintang presidential candidate, which was aimed at achieving the ultimate unification of one China.

=== Ma Ying-jeou administration ===
Lee Kuan Yew, the founding father of Singapore, long supported the ultimate unification of the two sides of the Taiwan Strait. In an interview with the Central News Agency in 2008, he believed that Ma Ying-jeou's proposition of no unification, no independence, no use of force improved cross-strait relations, but did not adhere to the Kuomintang's original ultimate unification stance.
In the 2012 presidential election, in 2011, Ma Ying-jeou proposed the Golden Decade Plan, which included a peace agreement with mainland China. The Democratic Progressive Party and the Taiwan Solidarity Union criticized the peace agreement for being based on the One China principle and aiming at ultimate unification. On 25 November 2011, James Soong, chairman of the People First Party, accepted an interview with the BBC and proposed that Taiwan should pursue ultimate unification in accordance with the Constitution of the Republic of China, criticizing Ma Ying-jeou for not restoring the Guidelines for National Unification. On the same day, when he accepted an interview with Next TV, he denied that his position was ultimate unification.
In 2012, the 18th National Congress of the Chinese Communist Party was held, and the 1992 Consensus was included in the political report for the first time. Xin Qi believes that the 1992 Consensus, which adheres to the one-China principle, can promote peaceful development across the Taiwan Strait, and the ultimate goal of peaceful development is peaceful reunification. In 2013, Wu Po-hsiung, honorary chairman of the Kuomintang, visited mainland China and met with Xi Jinping, general secretary of the Chinese Communist Party. He proposed that the Kuomintang promote the one China architecture, which was considered to be in line with the one China framework.
In 2014, the Ma Ying-jeou government promoted a minor adjustment to the high school curriculum guidelines, which was criticized by scholars such as Chang Yen-hsien for its purpose of promoting ultimate unification. Student groups and the Taiwan Solidarity Union that participated in the anti-curriculum adjustment movement also demanded the withdrawal of the minor curriculum adjustment for the same reason. In May of the same year, Shih Ming-teh, Su Chi, and others proposed the "New One-High School Principle," also known as the "University One-High School Framework." The TSU criticized it as a repackaging of Ma Ying-jeou's ultimate unification theory.

Planet Patrol (video game)

Planet Patrol is a 1982 horizontally scrolling shooter video game published by Spectravision for the Atari 2600. Players control a space ship sent to the fictional planet Spectra, tasked with destroying missile bases and rescuing stranded pilots. It is considered notable for scrolling from right to left, an usual arrangement some critics find to be disorientating. It also features a day and night cycle, with every other stage placed in the dark. It was one of first two games published by Spectravision, along with Gangster Alley, and one of the first five they published in Europe.
Critics had mixed opinions on Planet Patrol's gameplay and graphics. Some praised the graphics heavily, especially the backgrounds and day and night cycle. While others thought they could only be made up for by the exciting gameplay. Still others thought the gameplay was shallow and likely to get boring quickly, the lack of mobility a common criticism. One reviewer criticized the night stages for featuring "headache inducing" flashing.

== Gameplay ==
Planet Patrol is a horizontally scrolling shooter with a science fiction theme that exclusively scrolls right to left. Players control a spaceship sent to the planet Spectra to destroy enemy missile bases. While enemy ships (known as troublesome invading aliens or TIAs) fly past the player, their ship is stuck on one vertical axis and cannot be sped up or moved forward or backward. Enemies travel in straight lines going left to right. Near the end of a round, players get a chance to save a stranded pilot and destroy three missile bases. If they do not destroy the bases, they are destroyed by a force field. There is also a fuel gauge which can be refilled at the end of each round. Every other round, day turns into night and enemy space craft can only be seen briefly while the player fires a shot. There is a children's mode and an advanced mode along with a two player variation.

== Release ==
Spectravision first announced Planet Patrol, along with Gangster Alley, at Summer CES in June 1982. Both games were shipped within two weeks of the event. They were designed by an in-house studio. It was released in Europe in early 1983, including the United Kingdom,, West Germany, France,, and Italy. Spectravision would later be renamed to Spectravideo,  and develop the SV-318 home computer, a predecessor the Japanese MSX computer standard.

== Reception ==
Critics were mixed on Planet Patrol's graphics and sound. In Electronic Fun with Computers & Games, Frank Lovece thought it had "exceptional graphics", good pacing, and a designer who "must've devoured Frank Herbert's Dune trilogy in one sitting." Italian magazine Videogiochi was also impressed by the visuals. Electronic Games thought the sound and graphics were not "the most sophisticated available" but the intensity of the action makes up for that. French magazine Tilt also thought the gameplay makes up for poor sound and graphics. Joystik magazine called it "an involving fly-and-shoot space game with good, but not spectacular graphics." They considered the nighttime combat a nice touch.
Perry Greenberg in Video Games said it has "interesting variations" and called the nighttime flying "exciting". German magazine TeleMatch thought it could just as well have been a skiing game, and thought it was exciting but not outstanding game. TV Gamer thought it was a well thought out "very good space game." The Book of Atari Sotware called it a "challenger" that "will hold your interest for a while."
The Video Game Update did not recommend the game, considering the graphics "average" and the gameplay "likely to get boring after a while." They also considered the direction of travel from right to left disorienting. In 2007, Brett Weiss was very critical of the experience, calling it "shallow to the core", with "decent enough" graphics, "redundant" sound effects, "choppy" scrolling, "restrictive" gameplay, and "headache-inducing" flashing.

Tankard Inn

The Tankard Inn is a public house on Wetherby Road in Rufforth, a village in the City of York, England. Dating from the early 19th century and originally a house, it was remodelled in 1937 by architect H. Lane Fox for Samuel Smith's. The Campaign for Real Ale (CAMRA) rates its interior as of "very special national historic importance" with a two‑star grading. The local Historic Environment Record describes it as a community asset, though it is not a statutory asset of community value. The pub has experienced several closures and changes of management since 2019, reopening most recently in July 2026.

== History ==
The building dates from the early 19th century and is thought to have begun as a house before later enlargement. As Rufforth's only public house, it was extensively altered in 1937 when Samuel Smith's Brewery commissioned Leeds architect H. Lane Fox to remodel it. The Tankard Inn is regarded by the Campaign for Real Ale (CAMRA) as having an interior of "very special national historic importance" and is rated two stars in its grading scheme.
The local Historic Environment Record describes the Tankard Inn as a "community asset" and notes that it was put forward for inclusion on the Local List of Heritage Assets, although it is not designated as a statutory asset of community value as of October 2026.
The pub closed at Easter 2019 and remained shut for almost a year while Samuel Smith's sought new management, despite a recent refurbishment. It reopened in June 2023 under new tenants, but the tenancy ended after three months, with the brewery citing low sales; the managers stated that plans to introduce food could not proceed without investment in the kitchen. After another period of closure, the pub reopened in July 2026 under new management.

== Architecture ==
The building is a two‑storey structure with white render and a hipped roof of slate, the side slopes extending partway down the walls. There are chimneys at each end of the ridge. The ground floor has three bays, with a central entrance flanked by sets of three casement windows, the middle window in each group being slightly wider than the outer ones. The first floor contains two pairs of sash windows, and the left elevation has two additional casements. A shallow rectangular panel is recessed into the upper façade; the site of a former sign. To the rear are both two‑storey and single‑storey extensions, although their form is not clearly visible from the street.

=== Interior ===

The 1937 alterations created a compact two‑room layout that has changed little and is regarded as a good example of pub planning of the period. The central corridor was enlarged to form the public bar, given a modest Tudor treatment, and both rooms were fitted with matching three‑light leaded windows that set the 1930s character. Much of the pre‑war refurbishment survives, including the curving bar counter, bench seating and contemporary doors, although the lounge fittings and some of the wall panelling may be later additions.

Hokkaido Rensa Satsujin: Ohotsuku ni Kiyu

Hokkaido Rensa Satsujin: Ohotsuku ni Kiyu is a 1984 adventure game. The game has a mystery-theme where a man's body is discovered floating in Tokyo Bay, and the ongoing investigation pulls various isolated story points into the reveal of the culprit.
The game was part of a trilogy of mystery games by Yuji Horii, with the first being The Portopia Serial Murder Case (1983) and the latter being Karuizawa Yūkai Annai (1985). Rather than having players type out their commands such as "Open the Window" as seen in the original version of The Portopia Serial Murder Case, players can choose menu options to select specific commands in Hokkaido Rensa Satsujin. The game was first released for the PC-6001 and PC-88 in December 1984 and for various other Japanese home computers in 1985. The game sold 15,000 copies across all home computer models.
The game was later ported to the Family Computer in 1987 and was aimed at a younger audience. The game introduced some changes to the plot of the game one-third of the way in, as well as featured new characters and new art-style. The game has received various remakes such as Okhotsk ni Kiyu 98 for home computers in 1992 and The Hokkaido Serial Murder Case: The Okhotsk Disappearance ~Memories in Ice, Tearful Figurine~ in 2024.

== Gameplay ==
Hokkaido Rensa Satsujin: Ohotsuku ni Kiyu is an adventure game. It uses a command-selection system for its gameplay, rather than having players type in commands for character actions.. This allows for joystick control for all gameplay operations except when entering phone numbers or character names. As you gather information from various sources, the clues that were previously just isolated story points gradually begin to connect.

== Plot ==
In Hokkaido Rensa Satsujin: Ohotsuku ni Kiyu, a man's body is found floating at Harumi Pier in Tokyo Bay. Their identity is unknown. The setting moves from Tokyo to Hokkaido. Second and third murders occur one after another in Kushiro, Abashiri, Sapporo, Monbetsu, and Yubari.
There are two iterations of the game. The original version for home computers released in 1984 and 1985 and versions based on the Family Computer (Famicom) version from 1987 change the last third of the game feature new story content and a new identity of the culprit.

== Production ==
After working on The Portopia Serial Murder Case (1983), Yuji Horii branched into the genre of Japanese erotic games called eroge with Karuizawa Yūkai Annai (1985). While Horii worked on Karuizawa Yūkai Annai alone, he also worked on Hokkaido Rensa Satsujin: Ohotsuku ni Kiyu which was programmed by Toshiyuki Ueno. Ueno was a writer for Login magazine at the time. The game is considered the second game in a trilogy of mystery-themed games by Horii, which started with The Portopia Serial Murder Case and ended with Karuizawa Yūkai Annai. It became one of the only two games produced under the "Login Software" brand, with the other being Eiyū Yamato Takeru (1985).
Location scouting for Hokkaido Rensa Satsujin took place in November 1983. Horii said that Hokkaido Rensa Satsujin took longer to create than Karuizawa Yūkai Annai, as it was a collaboration. Login published articles about the game's production status in 1984. Horii only focused on developing the story for this game. He focused on making the plot unfold in a way that reveals the entire scope of the mystery, rather than relying on the unexpected identity of the culprit as in The Portopia Serial Murder Case. The game features graphics that feature close-ups and wide shots that resemble a manga comic. Horii said he did not do this intentionally, as he said it just came out of a normal part of his workflow.
Rather than having players type out their commands as seen in the original version of The Portopia Serial Murder Case, players can choose menu options to select specific commands in Hokkaido Rensa Satsujin. Horii said this was done after seeing people struggle with the command system in the original game due to the various words that could be used for commands in Japanese, so he opted for this menu option.

== Release ==
Hokkaido Rensa Satsujin: Ohotsuku ni Kiyu was released in Japan on December 21, 1984, for the PC-6001 and PC-88. It was released the following year for the PC-9801, Fujitsu FM-7 and lastly the MSX line of home computers in 1985. While the PC-88 version uses a numeric keypad for input, the PC-6001 and MSX versions let players control the game with a full keyboard, including number keys. Shinichi Yamoto of 4gamer.net described the game as an extraordinary success for the computer game market in Japan was it was released with the game selling 15,000 copies across all PC models. This was during an era when selling 5,000 copies of a game would have it be considered a hit game.
The version released for the Family Computer, or Famicom for short, was aimed at a younger audience. The Famicom port featured character designs by illustrator Arai Kiyokazu, which changed the style from to a more shonen manga-like art style. An initial idea for this version of the game was to have a vehicle-themed game when moving from location to location.  The last third of the game was rewritten with a focus on elaborating the character of Makiko. Other additions include a first-person maze section, as well as giving the game a full soundtrack. It was released on June 27, 1987.
A new character named Megumi was added to this version of the game. At Horii's suggestion, there is a secret in the game that allows the player to remove the bath towel from Megumi during the game. This led to debates among the developers on how to display her breasts. Initially, they planned to use a pink dot, but abandoned that idea and opted for one developer described as "a blank dot." Ultimately, depending on the configurations of the players television at the time, the dot appeared differently for players depending on their set-up.

=== Remakes ===
In 1992, a version with new graphics titled Okhotsk ni Kiyu 98 was published as a book bundled with the software as part of the Login Disk&Book series. This version features more realistic illustrations of the scenes and has a slightly different story in the latter half of the game that is based on the Famicom version of the title.
In the early days of mobile phone games, companies provided content such as wallpapers and game services for a fixed monthly fee. Versions of Hokkaido Rensa Satsujin were released in this format for various mobile services in Japan, such as i-αppli on October 15, 2002, and the following year on EZweb and J-Sky on June 18 and July 16, 2003, respectively.
A remake of the game titled The Hokkaido Serial Murder Case: The Okhotsk Disappearance ~Memories in Ice, Tearful Figurine~ was developed by G-Mode and released for home computers through Steam on September 11, and on the Nintendo Switch on September 12, 2024. The remake includes the Famicom port of the original game.

== Reception ==

Reviewing the Family Computer version, one reviewer in Famicom Hisshoubon said that some elements were improved from the original computer game such as the reveal of the culprit not feeling as rushed. Another reviewer
Another reviewer complimented the addition of background music, the movement of the screen saying that these novel elements would be appealing to players. The reviewer said that until now, they were not interested in adventure games, but became more drawn into this one due to the writing of Yuji Horii.

== Legacy ==
At the time, most adventure games required players to manually input all commands, such as "Open the window," and had an element of word-finding. Hokkaido Rensa Satsujin: Ohotsuku ni Kiyu was among the early video games to utilize a command-selection form of menu selection in adventure games. While this form of menu selection was previously seen in earlier Role-playing games, a small number of earlier adventure games such as Joshiryo Panic and Miko to Akemi no Jungle Adventure, Hokkaido Rensa Satsujin would only display the necessary commands when needed in the game. Japanese video game journalist Koji Fukuyama described it as creating a more formalized system for selecting commands in Japanese adventure games.
Using shortcuts for menu selections did not appear in the original The Portopia Serial Murder Case (1983),  but was incorporated in later versions and ports such as the version for the Sharp X1.
The art style of the Famicom version of Hokkaido Rensa Satsujin became the standard art style for adventure games on the console.

Felipe Reyes (artist)

Felipe Reyes (born 1944) is an American Chicano artist from San Antonio, Texas.
Reyes was the primary founder and organizer of the Con Safo art group, said to be one of the earliest, longest-lived, and most important of the Chicano art groups. It began with six members in 1968. The other founding members were: Jose Esquivel, Jesse Almazán, Roberto Ríos, Jose Garza, and Jesse "Chista" Cantú. Originally known as El Grupo or Grupo Seis, it went through two name changes before adopting the name Con Safo in late 1971.  The group became a vehicle for understanding Chicano identity, for learning how to create Chicano Art, and for developing exhibition opportunities when they were excluded from mainstream venues. Reyes himself developed as a Chicano artist within the group. He left Con Safo permanently in December of 1973. Reyes had moved from San Antonio to attend graduate school at the University of Michigan.

Reyes talked in 2009 about how Chicanos differed from Mexicans, and how Chicanos related to other historically disenfranchised groups: "Our art viewed Chicano issues as the condition of man: we were like other disenfranchised cultures with similar circumstances and experience. We made parallels with artists from their periods who had addressed social issues in their art. Rather than accepting our marginalization, we wanted full participation in society. We wanted to give expression to our experiences with directness and honesty. Our art was a mirror which reflected our bicultural realities. Mexicans are our brothers and we love them, we are descended from them--but we are also Americans, and we had to capture the uniqueness of our situation. At first we were naive, but our political awareness became deeper and more profound."
Artist Cesar Martinez moved to San Antonio in 1971 and soon met Reyes. Martinez, who would later become a Con Safo group member, said this of Reyes: "Though we were all trying to express these new ideas that were entering our political and cultural consciousness... I would say that Felipe’s work was the most politically on target, very topical."
Reyes has a B.A. in Art and Art  History, as well as a B.F.A. degree in Communication Design. His M.F.A. degree, in Painting, Drawing, and Art History, is from the University of Michigan.
During his time at Michigan, Reyes, along with Santos Martinez, another San Antonio native who was a member of the Con Safo group, played a part in inspiring the Raza Art and Media Collective (RAMC). In 1974, prior to RAMC's first publication, Reyes contributed to a  University of Michigan Movimiento Estudiantil Chicanx de Aztlán (MEChA) newsletter called Art and La Raza. Reyes is presently represented by the Aanna Reyes Gallery in San Antonio.

=== Early Chicano art period ===
Reyes focused on Chicano art from 1968 through 1973. One of Reyes' most influential works is Sacred Conflict (1971), a painting that initiated a critique of the commonly-held symbolism associated with the Alamo, a former Spanish mission in downtown San Antonio that figured in the Texas rebellion against Mexico. In Reyes' painting, a United Farm Workers (UFW) flag flies from the Alamo church. Reyes provided his interpretation of the painting in a Chicano publication called Magazín in 1972: “Throughout this painting is an intermingling of ironic symbols. The Alamo means different things to different peoples. To the Anglos it represents what they call oppression by Mexican tyrants. But to the Chicanos the Alamo is the symbol of Anglo aggression. The Chicano flag over it represents our viewpoint, that is, that the Alamo was a victory for us, the Chicanos. Yet the Anglos claim the whole event as their vic­tory. In a way the Alamo is a sacred symbol. It is an event trapped in the past, it is something beyond our manipulations and therefore something beyond criticism. But the different attitudes force me to apply meaning to this symbol. To be brief, the Alamo to me represents ven­geance for the Chicano, and the flag is the Chicano symbol for justice.”
The 1972 Magazín piece featured six paintings by Reyes (which are discussed here), a lithograph, and a page of the artist's commentary.
Sacred Conflict inaugurated the exhibition "The Other Side of the Alamo: Art Against the Myth," held at the Guadalupe Cultural Arts Center in San Antonio in 2018, and the painting's influence was discussed in several reviews, as well as subsequent discussions of the exhibition.
The Chicano Movement was deeply associated with and linked to the United Farm Workers, and Reyes made a number of other artworks that treated the UFW, including Southwesterly Winds (1970), whose title references the Bob Dylan lyric “You don’t need to be a weatherman to know which way the wind blows.” Reyes said that hippies were sympathetic to the Chicano movement, and he compared them to Pachucos.
Reyes also treated the UFW in other art works, including Todos Son Carnales (We Are all Brothers) (1971), Lettuce Prey (c. 1971-72), and ¡Huelga! (Strike) (1972). The Con Safo group organized an exhibition in support of the UFW that was meant to travel across the country and conclude at the UFW's national headquarters in La Paz, California, but much of the group's work was lost at the first traveling venue, in Mission, Texas in late 1972 or early 1973.
One of the artist's most provocative paintings is Instant Genocide, also known as Peace on Earth, Good Will Towards Men (c. 1971). According to an art historian who has studied Reyes' work, "This painting had an electrifying effect on everyone who saw it. The spray can’s design is a wrap-around Texas flag... 'INSTANT GENOCIDE,' the product name, is emblazoned across the blue and red fields... Directions are specified in small print: “To Be Used on Chicanos, Indians, Blacks, and all Other Undesirables."
Around 2020, Reyes discussed his rationale for the piece: "The practice of killing people of color was widespread." By utilizing an insecticide-like can, Reyes implied that people of color were regarded as subhuman pests, such as insects. He also addressed the role of the Ku Klux Klan and the Texas Rangers in violence, physical and social suppression, and what he called "cultural genocide." In 2025, Instant Genocide was illustrated and discussed in a Whitney Museum of American Art catalogue treating Chicano art in Texas.
Reyes also did cartoonish, satiric work, such as El Lambiache (the Spanish title is derived from the word for lick, so it could be translated as The Kiss-Ass, Ass-Licker, or Brown-Noser), a painting from c. 1972-73. This painting depicts a brown man wearing whiteface, with red balls at the end of his nose and on his cheek that liken him to a clown. The depicted person has a long, extended, undulating tongue that was influenced by Ed “Big Daddy” Roth's character called Ratfink. Reyes has described this character as a "sell out," a "Tio-taco" (uncle Tom), an "informant," and a "saboteur," one who scurries away when the lights are turned on (thus by implication a rat). This figure has blue eyes and is depicted against a US flag, one of whose stars has been replaced by a "UFW eagle emblem. When Reyes  interviewed Texas-born Chicana artist Carmen Lomas Garza in the December, 1971 issue of Magazín, he stated: "Institutions try to impose cultural imperialism on Chicanos. They try to make brown people into brown whites."
Reyes also made art works that referenced political events in the cities of Pharr and San Antonio. At Pharr in 1971, a bystander who emerged from a barber shop was shot and killed by police, who fired live rounds to disperse a demonstrating crowd. Reyes responded with a painting of a Texas flag that was bleeding from gunshot wounds, which he titled Pharr (1971).
In his 1972 painting titled Fast Buckaroo Politics, Reyes criticized San Antonio's political machine, known as the Good Government League (GGL), which dominated elections for many years. It featured a mock dollar bill, with the inscription "I BACK MAC," a reference to the machine boss Mayor McAllister, who was also a powerful banker. It featured a shield in the center like that of Superman with a large "S"--but the S was transformed into a dollar sign. It also mockingly referenced a GGL slogan: "Pave for progress," and it introduced a new slogan "In GGL we trust."
In another painting, called The Rising Star of San Juan, c. 1971-72, Reyes poked fun at GGL politician John Gatti, who became the new mayor of San Antonio. He opposed Gatti's ascendency to the rising sun of the UFW flag.
According to the Express-News in 2021, the Centro Aztlan's annual Virgin of Guadalupe exhibition was inaugurated 25 years earlier with a display of work by Reyes.
Speaking of the ambitions of Chicano artists, Reyes declared: "We created a kind of American art called Chicano art... We wanted to be honest and leave something good in the world."

=== Post Con Safo group art ===
In a September 24, 1974 letter to José Esquivel, Santos Martínez reported that Reyes had become an abstract artist while attending the University of Michigan. After Reyes returned to San Antonio, he discarded the Chicano works he had retained.
A May 30, 1993 article in the San Antonio Express-News, titled "Artistry vs. political activism," says the "issue of formal quality vs. political activism" is currently raging. The article's author contrasts Mel Casas and Reyes, stating Casas "is clearly a Chicano painter, while Reyes is known for a more abstract, introspective, formal style that does not feature an explicit political content." The author does not appear to be aware of Reyes political paintings, or of his involvement in the group. Reyes made several efforts before he was able to persuade Casas to join the Chicano art group.
