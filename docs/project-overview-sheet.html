From first principles

the chain we are testing
Prediction is compressiona probability p costs −log₂ p bits to transmit (Shannon 1948)
Part 1: What the identity givestheorems and published measurements
  1. Step 1Code length of a text under model Q is the sum of −log₂ q over its tokens
  2. Step 2Its expectation is H(P) + KL(P‖Q): entropy of the text plus the model's distance from it
  3. Step 3On fixed text H(P) cancels, so Δbits = ΔKL: a difference in code length is a distance between beliefs
  4. Step 4Pretraining minimises exactly this; across base models it tracks benchmarks at r = −0.93 (Huang 2024)
Part 2: What post-training doesour experiments
  1. Step 5Its objective moves Q toward a target, never toward general text, so KL to pretraining can only rise
  2. Step 6It does rise, in 16 of 16 stages and pairs: +0.1 % to +43 % (exp01, exp03)
  3. Step 7The rise is mostly new beliefs, not sharper ones: one temperature undoes ≤ 35 % (exp02)
  4. Step 8The rise measures distance moved, not skill gained; whether distance predicts forgetting is exp04, running
Thesis under testFresh-text bits per byte is a 10-second ruler for how far post-training moved a model from its pretraining distribution. If that distance predicts lost general ability and not gained target skill, it replaces the forgetting half of every post-training evaluation.
Steps 1 to 3 are algebra. Step 4 is someone else's measurement. Steps 5 to 7 are ours. Step 8 decides whether any of it saves money.
